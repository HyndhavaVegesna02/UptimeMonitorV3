# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Operators responsible for service health use this workspace during routine checks and active incidents. They need to identify what changed, determine the affected component or signal, approve a proposed public-status change when needed, and schedule or inspect maintenance without losing context.

## Product Purpose

Uptime Monitor V3 turns synthetic-monitor observations into a controlled operational view. Its internal frontend exposes the current six workflows: dashboard, availability, approvals, check history, maintenance, and publications. Success is a faster, more confident path from a signal to an informed action, using only the backend data that actually exists.

## Brand Personality

Decisive, tactile, composed. The interface should feel like a well-made field instrument: information-rich and calm under pressure, with a visual point of view that helps operators orient themselves instead of imitating a generic SaaS dashboard.

## Anti-references

- Identical KPI-card grids, decorative charts, and invented operational metrics.
- Purple-neon cyberpunk, default glass panels, and empty visual spectacle.
- Marketing-style hero layouts or page-load choreography in an operator tool.
- Color-only status communication, icon-only actions without accessible labels, and motion that distracts from active work.

## Design Principles

- Let the signal lead. Health, recency, and operational state should be easy to scan before secondary detail.
- Give every route its own working rhythm while keeping controls and states consistent across the application.
- Use material contrast and deliberate spatial structure to make the product memorable without obscuring real data.
- Preserve the wire truth. Missing backend data is shown honestly, never represented by fabricated content.
- Treat incident work as focused interaction. Motion should clarify a state change and collapse under reduced-motion preferences.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG AA contrast, preserve keyboard-first operation and semantic controls, never communicate status by color alone, retain light and dark themes, and honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
